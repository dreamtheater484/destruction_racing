# Reference Board (M0)

The visual reference board for the art direction (plan §7.4). Every art gate compares our renders against it.

- **Links only.** No images are saved or embedded. If you want local copies, say so and we save them with licence info (plan §7.4).
- **Licences** are given for the period photos. Game screenshots and videos are copyrighted and are for **internal reference only**. Never trace or ship them.
- **Real car models** are named in §4 for internal reference only. The game uses the fictional names from §9.1.
- Compiled on 2026-10-14. ✱ = not verified directly (for example, the site blocks automated access, or the link came from a search index and the page was not opened).

### 1.3 Where to look, by topic

- **The Bowl and arenas:** the DD1 Bowl (Dosgamert, Tremenator, the wiki page), then the DD2 bowls (World of Longplays [246]: Red Pike, Colosseum, Pit, Death Bowl).
- **Crossover tracks:** Old Nerd #20 CrossOver and the Crossover wiki page. In DD2, the S.C.A. Motorplex is the crossover.
- **HUD with damage diagram:** the MobyGames DOS galleries and BrenFu's Bowl practice. Compare the DD2 PC and PS versions; the wiki notes that the PC damage indicator is less detailed.
- **Liveries and roof colours:** the competitor pages and the PS longplays (higher colour depth). DD2 cars are simpler, showing mostly just the car number.
- **Smoke and damage states:** ZxSilentKLxZ's invincible Bowl, Old Nerd's CrossOver, and the DD2 PC captures (flying hoods, fire).

## 5. What to take from this

1. **Palette: saturated cars against a muted world.** The real derbies and DD both put primary red, yellow, blue, green and white bodies against ochre dirt, grey concrete and faded grass. Keep the environment's saturation low so the liveries carry the colour.
2. **Lighting: high sun with hard shadows, then golden hour.** The 1972 DOCUMERICA speedway and the 1981 Ohio fair photos show flat, bright daylight with a hazy sky. DD1's Bowl ran in day and dusk. Backlit dust and smoke is the money shot.
3. **Liveries are hand-made, not factory-made.** Big door and roof numbers, brushed or spray-canned; local-garage sponsors; mismatched panels (primer-grey doors, a hood from another car); crude names and slogans painted on (the "Jaws" car). Our livery layers in §9.4 should produce imperfect edges and drips, not vector-perfect stickers.
4. **Roof colour as rank.** BriSCA-style stock cars use roof colours to grade drivers, which matches DD's division roofs. Keep roofs flat, bright and readable from the chase camera and from above.
5. **Derby-prep details sell authenticity.** Glass removed, tied-down or chained hoods and trunks, a number board on the roof, a cage visible through the windows, headlights taped or cut out, chrome trim stripped.
6. **The Bowl's dressing.** Tyre walls (stacked and often painted white), concrete blocks and earth banks, wooden boards and wire fencing, grandstands behind a crowd rail, floodlight poles, a water truck wetting the floor, a tow truck or loader pulling wrecks, flaggers and marshals. **Mountains beyond and a hot-air balloon**, as in DD1.
7. **Infield life (Crossroads Speedway).** Pit trailers, campers, pickups, flags, a scoreboard tower and fair signage. The DD2 Motorplex and the Ontario County Fair photos are the template.
8. **Damage reads as silhouette first.** Front and rear ends crushed back to the wheels (derby drivers reverse into each other to protect the engine), trunks "tented" upward, roofs and pillars bent, bumpers hanging by one corner, wheels toed in, hoods missing (DD2). Then add the Wreckfest detail on top: sharp crease lines, chipped paint revealing primer and bare metal, cracked glass, scraped edges.
9. **Smoke tells the health story.** DD and the real derbies both show white steam (radiator) giving way to grey, then black oily smoke and fire as a car dies. Use it as a readable health cue alongside the HUD (pillar 5).
10. **Dirt and mud build up.** Wet-down arenas throw mud up the sills and wheel arches, and dry ones throw dust plumes (the 1973 Polygoon newsreel and the Ohio 1981 photos). Wreckfest 2's mud build-up is the quality target. Ruts and skid marks should accumulate over a heat.
11. **HUD: a 90s broadcast look, kept simple.** DD's top-down car damage diagram with coloured zones, chunky numerals for position and points, and nothing else on screen. Modernise it with clean type and a subtle TV-graphics frame, but keep the diagram as the hero element.
12. **The Wreckfest bar for the car models.** Panel gaps as geometry, separate bumpers, hood and doors that detach as physics debris, believable tyre sidewalls and rims, and interiors visible through broken glass. Compare our photo-studio turntables against the Digital Foundry Wreckfest 2 footage at every art gate.
